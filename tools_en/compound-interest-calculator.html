What is compound interest?

With compound interest, the interest you earn is added to your capital and itself starts earning interest. The basic formula is final amount = capital × (1 + r)n, where r is the annual return and n the number of years. The longer the period, the stronger the effect: €10,000 at 5% grows to about €16,289 gross in 10 years and about €26,533 in 20 years.

How much tax is paid on interest in Italy?

In Italy interest and financial gains are taxed at 26% (deposit accounts, current accounts, funds, ETFs, shares, corporate bonds). Italian government bonds and those of "white list" countries, such as BTP and BOT, are taxed at 12.5%. On deposit accounts the tax is withheld directly by the bank.

What is the 0.20% stamp duty?

Financial products (deposit accounts, securities, funds, ETFs) pay an annual stamp duty (imposta di bollo) of 0.20% of their value. Current accounts and savings books pay a fixed €34.20 a year instead, only if the average balance is above €5,000. The calculator applies 0.20% to the balance at the end of each year.

What is the rule of 72?

It is a quick way to see how many years it takes to double your money: divide 72 by the annual return. At 3% it takes about 24 years, at 6% about 12 years. Remember that tax, fees and inflation reduce the real return.

Where can I find government bond yields?

Up-to-date BTP yields are on the website of the Italian Treasury Department and on Borsa Italiana. To compare deposit accounts you can use online comparison sites.
