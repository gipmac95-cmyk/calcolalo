Tips for the right price

  • Look at completed sales, not listings: what counts is the price the game actually sold for.
  • Only compare copies in the same condition and the same version (PAL or NTSC, special or standard edition).
  • For retro games the box and manual make a big difference: a complete copy can be worth twice the cartridge alone.

Why don't you give a fixed price?

Used video game values change a lot and fast: new editions, reprints and collecting trends can push prices up or down within weeks. That's why the most reliable price is the average of recent real sales of the same game in the same condition. The calculator automatically removes outliers (less than half or more than double the median).

Where is the best place to sell?

Private listing sites such as Subito and Vinted often get you the most. Platforms with fees such as eBay reach more buyers, but you have to subtract the fee. Second-hand shops pay on the spot, but much less than market value.
